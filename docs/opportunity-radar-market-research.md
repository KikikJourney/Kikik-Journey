# Opportunity Radar: independent market-demand research

**Research snapshot:** 10 October 2026  
**Purpose:** compare internal Opportunity Radar hypotheses against independent market evidence. This is market context, not a list of buyers and not proof that a particular offer will sell.

## Independent evidence

1. **Bank Indonesia / BI Institute — national digital-adoption study.** The study covers 10,142 ultra-micro, micro, and small enterprises across 34 provinces. It examines adoption of e-procurement, POS, e-marketing, e-commerce, and digital payments and reports that adoption is associated with business performance, innovation, financial literacy, and inclusion. Source: [Bank Indonesia study summary](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/kajian/Pages/A-Study-Of-National-Digital-Adoption-Index-For-Indonesian-Umses-%28Expansion%29.aspx).
2. **Bappenas — Striving to Thrive (March 2025).** Survey of 827 Indonesian micro and small enterprises across sectors including food and beverage, fashion, and crafts. The executive summary identifies uneven revenue outcomes and challenges including access to credit, market expansion, low digital-tool adoption, and sustainability practices. Source: [Bappenas report PDF](https://komens.bappenas.go.id/public/storage/files/Dt.5.4_Striving_to_Thrive_The_state_of_Indonesian_Micro_and_Small_Enterprises_202425_2025.pdf).
3. **BPS — Profile of Micro and Small Industry 2024.** Official statistical publication covers business characteristics, capital and finance, constraints, innovation, internet use, and marketing distribution. Source: [BPS publication](https://www.bps.go.id/id/publication/2025/09/16/a83f105e49377d0a7434e62a/profil-industri-mikro-dan-kecil-2024.html).
4. **Google, Temasek, and Bain — e-Conomy SEA 2025, Indonesia summary.** Reports that Indonesia's digital economy was projected to approach US$100 billion GMV in 2025, with e-commerce projected at US$71 billion and substantial video-commerce growth. This is ecosystem-level context, not evidence of demand for a particular small automation service. Source: [Google Indonesia summary](https://blog.google/intl/id-id/company-news/outreach-initiatives/e-conomy-sea-2025-ekonomi-digital-indonesia-mendekati-gmv-us100-miliar/).

## What this evidence supports—and what it does not

- It supports investigating operational digitization, sales/market access, recordkeeping, and workflow friction among Indonesian micro and small businesses.
- It does **not** establish that all UMKM have the same pain, can pay for custom automation, use Google Sheets/WhatsApp in the same way, or prefer the current Kikik Journey price points.
- Macro reports are context evidence. A prospect is not qualified until a public page contains a specific, current problem and a relevant request or sufficiently clear pain signal. A lead is not outreach-ready without a suitable public contact channel and a defensible offer fit.
- GitHub issues and automation-community threads can expose explicit technical requests, but they skew toward technically literate users and should not be treated as representative of Indonesian UMKM.
- Public business websites and sector-specific queries broaden coverage, but search results are still a convenience sample and can be affected by indexing, language, search-engine blocking, and site accessibility.

## Cross-validation protocol used by Opportunity Radar

1. Search a diversified query bank: Indonesian UMKM pain hypotheses, active automation-community requests, and public business-web signals across multiple sectors.
2. Inspect source pages, not search snippets alone. Preserve source URL, query, title, evidence excerpt, source domain, and public contact channel.
3. Keep three states separate: **QUALIFIED** (explicit request evidence), **WATCH** (relevant pain without enough purchase-intent evidence), and **REJECT** (insufficient evidence or irrelevant page).
4. Never convert a macro report, repository popularity score, inferred email, or AI recommendation into a buyer or sale.
5. Track evidence by source domain and query, and enforce a bounded page-inspection budget to avoid expensive or stalled scheduled runs.
6. Validate willingness to pay through actual one-to-one buyer responses, quoted scope, accepted offers, paid orders, refunds, and delivery outcomes. Until those events exist, price and conversion assumptions remain hypotheses.
